01 / Research Focus

Humanoid Robots
That Move Like Us

My research sits at the intersection of control theory, machine learning, and biomechanics — aiming to give humanoid robots the agility, efficiency, and robustness of human locomotion.

🦾

Humanoid Control

RL sim-to-real transfer, Model Predictive Control, and Whole-Body Control for dynamic, contact-rich humanoid locomotion across varied terrains.

🏗️

Robot Development

End-to-end hardware involvement: design validation, system identification for robots with kinematic loops, and electrical system design including PCB layout.

🦶

Actuated-Toe Locomotion

Pioneering actuated-toe enabled humanoid locomotion using imitation learning — inspired by human biomechanics for greater energetic efficiency.

🎬

Motion Capture Retargeting

Custom fast SQP solver for MoCap retargeting at 20,000 fps — 100× faster than GMR/ProtoMotion baselines, completing 4-hour datasets in under 1 minute.

🧠

Sim-to-Real Transfer

Bridging the gap between simulation and physical hardware for robots with complex kinematic loops and cooperative actuation schemes.

⚡

Trajectory Optimization

Re-targeting human MoCap data to kinodynamically feasible, hardware-consistent robot trajectories via custom optimization frameworks.

About My Work

I am a PhD candidate in the DARoS Lab at UMass Amherst, working under Prof. Donghyun Kim. My dissertation focuses on efficient humanoid locomotion — specifically using actuated toes to replicate the energy-saving mechanics of human walking.

Outside of robotics research, I am deeply interested in the intersection of biomechanics and machine learning — and how the principles of human motion can inform the design of more capable robotic systems.
